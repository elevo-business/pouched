01 — Positioning

The idea

One material that adapts to you, instead of the other way around. Flat when there’s nothing to carry. There the moment there is.

Everyone else sells a gadget with a party-trick. We sell the quiet end of it: an everyday object that solves the oldest carry problem — slim or enough room — by refusing to choose. That’s not marketing. It’s the product. Our whole job as a brand is to take it as seriously as it deserves.

02 — Name

Why joey

A joey is the one carried in the pouch.

A joey is a baby kangaroo — the thing a marsupial keeps close, protected, always within reach. The name turns the attention away from the mechanism and onto what you keep: your card, your key, the one thing you can’t lose. Warm, protective, precise. It works in German and English, it’s three letters longer than a logo needs to be, and it never says “kangaroo pouch” out loud — it just knows.

03 — Wordmark

The o is the pouch.

Lowercase, tight, confident — a warm grotesque, not a luxury serif. The single ownable move is the second letter: the o is drawn slightly heavier at its base and rounds into a pouch, as if something is resting inside it. In motion it swells; at rest it settles into a soft ellipse. That one detail is the whole brand in a glyph.

  • Clearspace = the height of the o on every side.
  • Never outline, gradient, stretch, or drop-shadow the mark.
  • Bone on ink, or ink on bone. The caramel is for accents — never the wordmark.

05 — Typography

One family, set well

07 — Art direction

Photography

The object, ernst genommen. Studio light, warm shadow, macro on the material. The hero shot is always the same moment: the pouch stretching, or snapping back. No festival wristbands, no grinning models — the product is the star.

HeroMacro. The pouch mid-stretch, a single card easing in. 85mm, one warm key light, deep shadow.
Empty vs. fullTwo frames, same case: flat and unnoticed / swelled and holding. The whole promise in a pair.
In handCropped tight — thumb pressing the pouch closed. Skin, silicone, texture. Never a full body.

Rule of thumb: if a competitor could run the same photo, reshoot it. Ours are recognisable from the light and the restraint alone.

08 — Packaging

Half the value is in the opening.

Matte ink box, debossed mark, no photo on the lid. Inside: the case seated in a warm bone tray, a single printed card, one sentence. Cents to produce, luxury to open — the same lever Apple pulls. The unboxing is the first time the brand touches the customer’s hands; it should feel heavier than €70.

Card copy: “Fill it, or don’t. It’s ready either way.”

In one paragraph

joey is an expandable phone case with a soft silicone pouch built into the back. Empty, it lies flat and forgotten. Fill it — a card, a key, a folded note — and it quietly swells to hold them. It replaces the wallet and, most days, the bag. Same clever object everyone’s discovering; taken seriously, made warm, and built to be kept.
